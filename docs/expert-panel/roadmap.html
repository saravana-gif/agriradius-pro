The farmer's question

"No one warned us about the failed rains." The warnings existed.

Official, free warnings were issued from mid-April onward. They stayed in English PDFs and press releases. IMD closed its 199 District Agromet Units in 2024, the teams that used to turn these bulletins into block-level advice, and nobody filled that gap. OneRoot's field staff can.

What a farmer in Chamarajanagar says he would have done with a May warning

Not sown 4 acres of maize on the first June shower (about ₹25,000 an acre saved), and sown short-duration ragi or horse gram later. Planted half the turmeric, mulched, and fixed the drip lines in May. Skipped the new tissue-culture banana batch. Serviced the borewell motor early. Bought ragi straw for the cows before the price doubled. The FPO would have ordered 60% of its inputs, not 100%.

Farmer and FPO president, 35 years, 12 acres, 900-member FPO

The panel's conclusion: most of the harm could have been flagged by measuring what was already happening (rain to date against normal, dry-spell days, crop stage, tank and reservoir levels) plus rules that institutions have already written, such as CRIDA's district contingency plans. Long-range prediction plays a smaller, bounded role. The missing piece was delivery: in Kannada or Tamil, for a named hobli, carried by a person the farmer trusts.

What all nine agreed

Design principles

Measure first, predict carefully

About 80% of the work is monitoring: rain against normal, dry spells, crop stage, water, prices. Prediction appears in three places only: a seasonal odds card for the decision table, the 0–15 day forecast for sowing, spraying and drying, and crop lags such as coconut. District forecasts 3–4 weeks out score about as well as chance.

Compute overnight, read instantly

Background jobs on the existing server pre-compute every watched village. Opening a village card never triggers an Earth Engine run. One weekly batch uses far less quota than dozens of clicks.

Say the odds, keep score

"7 in 10 chance the season ends below normal; a usual year is 3 in 10", with the issue date and source. Never "it will". Every alert is logged permanently and checked afterwards. A monthly "we said / it happened" board at FPO meetings is how trust builds.

Advice stays separate from sales

Farmer-facing cards name practices and nutrients only: no brand, no product, no OneRoot price, with the source cited (CRIDA, UAS, TNAU, KVK). Input stocking and sourcing use the same signals on internal views only.

Match the government's yardstick

Drought alerts mirror the Drought Manual 2020: rainfall triggers shown separately from crop-impact triggers. In 2023 about 40 Karnataka taluks with a rain deficit were not declared because crops stayed green, so rain alone over-warns.

Staff logs beat datasets

Within two seasons, structured field logs (sowing dates, borewells, harvest counts, buyer rates) will be more accurate than any public dataset for your villages.

From the farmer and the field-ops head

Every alert must pass these ten checks

  1. Names a hobli or village, not a district.
  2. Gives one action for this week, its cost, and what happens if ignored.
  3. States honest odds against the usual year, with issue date and source.
  4. Fits one share card or a one-minute voice note, and opens offline.
  5. The officer can explain why in one sentence, with three questions to ask.
  6. No brand and no OneRoot sale in farmer-facing text.
  7. The action is actually available: seed in stock, labour, water.
  8. A named officer delivers it within 48 hours and logs the acknowledgement.
  9. It is scored after 7 days; rules right less than about 60% of the time are retired.
  10. Viewing it needs no button press and no Earth Engine run.

For the owner

Eight questions that change the plan

  1. Scale: how many villages and officers in the first season? 30–50 fits manual WhatsApp forwarding and the Earth Engine quota; 500 needs a different design.
  2. Earth Engine registration: is the project registered under the NGO's name, and who renews its noncommercial verification each year?
  3. When an alert fires, what can OneRoot actually offer? Short-duration or millet seed in stock, credit, buy-back for alternative crops? And what does OneRoot do if a warning was wrong and a farmer lost a crop?
  4. Sign-off: does a OneRoot agronomist sign the advice alone, or with KVK / UAS / TNAU review and credit? Do you commit to no brand or sale in farmer-facing cards?
  5. Tender coconut: tall (Maddur type) or dwarf / hybrid palms? Do staff record each farm's harvest date and nut count today? Does OneRoot also trade copra?
  6. Chilli: which belt matters most (rainfed Byadgi, irrigated Ballari / Raichur, or Tamil Nadu mundu), and do you buy dry or green chilli?
  7. Turmeric mapping: can you commit about 20 field-days per region this October–December to label 600+ fields?
  8. Continuity: will the same officers cover the same villages next year, and will each FPO get its village data back? The farmer said a system that disappears after one season is worse than none.
